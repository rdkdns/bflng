Conclusion

The phytopathogenic state of the examined sample is characterised by a high level of biotic stress for agricultural crops, due to the considerable spread of soil and leaf pathogens. The ratio of specific (9) to universal (9) pathogens indicates that the causal agents are highly adapted to the agroecosystem, which can cause long-term damage to crops regardless of changes in crop rotation.

A significant pathogenic load was detected in the examined soil sample, caused by a complex of fungal and bacterial causal agents that threaten a wide range of vegetable crops. Fungi of the genus Fusarium are especially numerous, in particular F. oxysporum, F. solani, F. graminearum, F. proliferatum and F. tricinctum. These agents can penetrate the vascular system of plants, causing systemic wilting, blockage of water transport, root rots and gradual exhaustion of the plants. Their life cycle is often maintained in the soil by chlamydospores, which allows them to persist in an inactive state between growing seasons.

Other pathogenic fungi complete the risk picture, in particular Alternaria alternata, Nectria ramulariae, Acremonium fusidioides, Clonostachys phyllophila and Leptodiscella africana. They cause leaf spots, necroses, rots of seed material, damage to generative organs and a general decrease in photosynthetic activity. All these changes directly affect the productivity and resilience of crops in open or protected ground.

Several bacterial pathogens were also identified, in particular Pantoea vagans, Acidovorax delafieldii, Rhodococcus fascians and Pseudomonas rhizosphaerae. Their activity leads to soft rots, galls, bacterial leaf spots and vascular diseases. These organisms adapt quickly to changes in the environment and often interact with fungi, forming mixed infections that are difficult to diagnose. Such a spectrum of pathogens creates a complex threat throughout the growing season.

Alongside the phytopathogens, a developed group of microorganisms with antagonistic properties was detected in the sample, which indicates the functional capacity of the soil for biological self-protection. The basis of the protective microbiome is formed by the fungi Trichoderma harzianum, Trichoderma stromaticum and Clonostachys rosea. These organisms are known as some of the most effective biocontrol agents against Fusarium, Rhizoctonia, Botrytis and other pathogens. They produce hydrolytic enzymes (chitinases, glucanases), form biofilms on the root surface and attack pathogens directly through mycoparasitism.

Antagonistic bacteria, in particular Bacillus simplex, Bacillus aryabhattai and Stenotrophomonas sp., actively produce secondary metabolites (antibiotics, surfactants) with antifungal and antibacterial action. They effectively suppress the development of causal agents in the root zone, displacing them through competition for substrates or by changing the chemical composition of the micro-ecosystem. These organisms also form stable biofilms, which increases their persistence and effectiveness in the soil.

The presence of antagonists indicates not only the ability to restrain the development of infections, but also a general potential for ecological balance. Their effectiveness is especially high against the pathogens that dominate the risk group in this sample, which supports the use of biological methods of crop protection.

Overall assessment

The overall analysis shows that, despite the presence of a wide spectrum of pathogens, the microbial ecosystem of the sample retains signs of a stable balance. The intensive action of antagonistic organisms, in particular Trichoderma spp. and Bacillus spp., compensates for the presence of causal agents and restrains the formation of a critical infectious background. The functional diversity of microorganisms in the sample is a sign of a mature soil microbiota, which provides dynamic stability even under external stress.

This balance allows a cautiously optimistic forecast: provided that agrotechnical hygiene, biological sanitation and a regulated crop rotation are observed, the risk of mass infection remains under control. At the same time, preserving biodiversity and monitoring the key pathogens are necessary conditions for long-term phytosanitary well-being. The microbiological structure of the sample creates a basis for ecologically balanced farming, with priority given to biological control over chemical intervention.

Notes

* The quantity is given as the number of copies per 1 kg of soil. The number next to each species of fungi or bacteria reflects the number of unique marker genes specific to that species that were read during the analysis: the number of cases in which a DNA fragment unambiguously corresponding to that species was detected in the sample.

This approach makes it possible not only to detect a specific species but also to assess its share in the overall structure of the microbiome. Even microorganisms present in very small quantities can be detected, which makes the method extremely sensitive. The higher the value, the higher the abundance of the species in the sample.

Since 250 mg of soil is used for the analysis, the number of markers per 1 kg of soil is calculated as: number of marker genes × (1000 / 0.25) = number of marker genes × 4000.
